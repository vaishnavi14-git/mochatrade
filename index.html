Market basics, without the jargon.

Learn one idea at a time. Finish each card to unlock the practice market.

01 • Price

The market price is simply the current price at which the simulated asset is trading.

Think: “What is one unit worth right now?”

02 • Long

A long position is a practice bet that the price will rise. If the simulated price rises after you enter, the position gains value.

Long = expecting ↑

03 • Short

A short position is a practice bet that the price will fall. If the simulated price falls after you enter, the position gains value.

Short = expecting ↓

04 • Margin

Margin is the virtual amount allocated to support a leveraged position.

More leverage can make gains and losses change faster.

05 • Liquidation

In this simulation, liquidation is a risk event where a leveraged position loses enough value that the simulator closes it.

Leverage does not remove risk.

06 • Fees

A real platform can charge trading fees. Here, fees are simulated so you can understand their effect.

Always know the cost before trading.

Questions? We've got you.

Short answers for someone opening a trading simulator for the first time.

Is this real trading?

No. This prototype is a game-like learning environment. The balance is virtual and the trades are simulated.

What is a long?

In this simulator, going long means you are practicing a scenario where you expect the simulated asset price to rise.

What is a short?

Going short means you are practicing a scenario where you expect the simulated asset price to fall.

Why does leverage exist?

Leverage lets a smaller margin control a larger simulated position. It also makes gains and losses change faster, so it is an important concept to learn carefully.

Can I lose real money?

Not in this prototype. It has no real-money wallet or payment flow.

What happens if I make a mistake?

That's the point. Reset the simulation, try a different decision, and compare the outcomes. Mistakes are part of the learning loop.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is an educational simulation and should not be treated as investment advice.
